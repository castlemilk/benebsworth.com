## Predict the next turn

The cyan arrow shows the dominant spin angular momentum along the axle. Gravity acts downward at the rotor centre. The amber horizontal arrow at the pivot shows gravitational torque, which points along the instantaneous change in angular momentum.

1. Select **Baseline**. A 0.4 kg solid disc of radius 0.08 m spins at 1200 rpm, with a 0.06 m centre-of-mass lever arm.
2. The slow-precession model predicts 1.464 rad/s, or a 4.29 s period.
3. Select **Double the spin**. The rate halves and the period doubles.
4. Select **Double the lever**. The rate doubles because gravitational torque doubles.
5. Return to Baseline and change **Tilt from vertical**. The precession rate stays fixed while the cone changes shape.

This workbench evaluates steady slow precession. It does not simulate arbitrary release conditions, nutation, friction, or a rotor running down. The rotor spokes are slowed tenfold for legibility. The precession follows physical time: one timeline pass is π seconds, then the scene restarts. Arrow lengths and the drawn lever arm are illustrative rather than a scale drawing.
