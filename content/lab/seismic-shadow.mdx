## Read the globe from the top

The quake is the white dot on top of the globe. Cyan rays stay in the mantle, violet rays (PKP) bend through the liquid outer core, amber rays (PKIKP) cross the solid inner core, and dim grey rays reflect from a core boundary. The sky-blue belt is the model's shadow: the gap between the last mantle ray and the first core ray, swept around the source-antipode axis.

### Five experiments

1. Scrub the timeline at the defaults. Direct P lands at 90° after 13.0 min and the last mantle ray reaches 98.4°. No violet ray lands before 145.1°, so the belt spans 98.4° to 145.1° against the observed 103°-143°.
2. Watch the amber rays land inside the belt from about 116°, the geometric onset of PKIKP. Inge Lehmann used such arrivals in 1936 to argue for a solid inner core.
3. Select **S waves: no arrivals beyond ≈103°**. Every ray that touches the core bounces (ScS), the belt becomes a cap and the readout gives 102.7°.
4. Select **Fast core ×1.5: shadow shrinks to 98°–108°**. PKP pulls in to 108.0° and the antipodal PKIKP time drops from 20.2 to 17.5 min.
5. Select **Small core 2800 km: a narrower, farther shadow**. The belt moves to 115.5°-153.6° and P at 90° slows to 13.3 min.

Every ray conserves the ray parameter

$$
p = \frac{r\,\sin i}{v},
$$

so the inner-core critical take-off sits near 5.8° from the vertical, and the model always draws it.

### What the model leaves out

Geometric rays in a spherically symmetric PREM cut into 120 power-law shells: no amplitudes, no diffraction, no converted phases such as SKS, and only totally reflected inner-core rays appear as PKiKP. Diffracted P and the PKP caustic move the observed edges to 103° and 143°. The source sits at the surface, and the core-radius control stretches the lower mantle and outer core onto the chosen boundary.
