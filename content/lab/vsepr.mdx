## Read the lobes as room, not electrons

The white sphere is the central atom. Cyan lines are bonding domains ending at ligand atoms on the faint direction sphere. Pink lobes are lone-pair domains, drawn wider as their repulsion weight grows. The bright amber arc spans the closest pair of bonds; the faint arc spans the lone-pair angle. The readouts always use the full weight.

The timeline ramps that weight from its full value at either end down to 1 at the midpoint, so the middle of the sweep shows the shape the molecule would have if lone pairs repelled like bonds.

### Five experiments

1. Select **Methane CH4**. Four equal domains settle at 109.47° and the energy reads 3.674, the Thomson minimum for four charges.
2. Return to the default water state. The bonds close to 104.40° while the lone pairs open to 114.71°; experiment says 104.48°.
3. Select **Ammonia NH3**. The same weight gives 106.69° against a measured 106.67°.
4. From the water state, drag **Electron domains N** to 6. The lobes jump to opposite poles and the bonds flatten into a square: xenon tetrafluoride, 90.00°, energy 11.681, a local minimum the trans seed selects (cis is 11.679).
5. Select **Sulfur tetrafluoride SF4**. The lone pair takes an equatorial seat and the axial fluorines lean away to 87.33°. Push **Lone-pair repulsion weight** to 1.5 and the seesaw folds further.

The scene minimises a weighted Thomson energy over unit vectors $\mathbf u_i$,

$$
E=\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_i\,w_j}{\lvert\mathbf u_i-\mathbf u_j\rvert},
$$

with $w=1$ for bonds and the slider value for lone pairs, using 200 steps of projected gradient descent from a nudged ideal seed.

### What the model leaves out

Domains are sizeless points, so bond lengths and lobe shapes are illustrative. The weight is fitted, not derived. Electronegativity, multiple bonds and d orbitals are absent, and the SF4 equatorial angle comes out wrong (123.6° against a measured 101.6°). The lone-pair slider is clamped to $N-2$ so two bonds always remain; the shape readout says when.
