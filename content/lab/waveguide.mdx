## A transverse pattern sets a propagation threshold

The cyan arrows show the $\mathrm{TE}_{10}$ electric field in an air-filled rectangular guide. The wide dimension is $a$, the height is $a/2$, and the displayed length is $2a$.

### Cross cutoff slowly

1. Select **Propagating**. The electric-field pattern advances down the guide.
2. Select **Near cutoff**. Its longitudinal wavelength becomes long, while the transverse half-sine remains fixed.
3. Select **Below cutoff**. The field changes sign in place and its amber envelope decays away from the entrance.

The frequency threshold and longitudinal dispersion are

$$
f_c=\frac{c}{2a},
\qquad
\beta^2=\left(\frac{2\pi f}{c}\right)^2-\left(\frac{\pi}{a}\right)^2.
$$

At $a=22.86\ \mathrm{mm}$, cutoff is approximately $6.557\ \mathrm{GHz}$. At $f/f_c=1.5$, group velocity is about $0.745c$. At $f/f_c=0.75$, the field envelope falls to about 1.57% after the displayed two-width length.

Changing **Wide wall** scales the physical geometry and the frequency readouts together. The drawing stays normalised. Changing **Frequency / cutoff** changes the actual field behaviour.

### Scope of the picture

The guide is perfectly conducting and lossless. Only $E_y$ is drawn; the magnetic field also has a longitudinal component. Below cutoff, the model shows a single decaying solution in a guide extending away from a driven entrance. It does not model transmission through a finite evanescent section.
