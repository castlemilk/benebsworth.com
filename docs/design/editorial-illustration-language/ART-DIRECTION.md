# A visual grammar for editorial illustrations

## The proposition

Draw a relationship that the reader can hold in mind while reading. The picture should make one aspect of an argument visible: an interface between parts, an interior behind a boundary, or a form changing along a continuous path. A new subject alone does not establish a new editorial idea.

This proposed language extends the existing graphite-and-ivory editorial objects into compositions with a clear focal event. It retains the blog's selected research folio and the beneb preference for substantial forms, strong contours and deliberate space. It does not transfer a selected logo, speculative brand mythology or the Hardware console's interface styling into an article.

The current specimens are assistant proposals awaiting Ben's review. They establish a repeatable way to brief and assess art, not a final approved house style.

## Composition

Begin with one sentence describing what changes, separates, reveals or connects. Choose a viewpoint that makes that relationship visible without a caption. The caption supplies its meaning and limits afterwards.

Use one dominant mass and one supporting counter-shape. Let the important negative space remain large enough to read when the image is reduced. In EL01 the interval between the two pieces is more important than the texture of either face. In EL02 the displaced section explains the exposed interior. In EL03 one connected form carries the eye across changes in height.

Landscape 3:2 is the starting canvas for this family. It supports a reading rhythm without becoming a masthead or a row of icons. Tall subjects may occupy less horizontal space, as in EL02. This is a composition choice, not permission to force every image into the same optical width.

Aim for 12-18% clear intrinsic margin around substantial painted forms. Treat 12% as the review floor, measured at alpha greater than 16/255; also inspect all nonzero alpha because a substantial-content bound can hide faint residue. The new r2 images meet the 12% substantial-content floor, but not the stricter 16% correction target on every side. They retain faint low-alpha residue, recorded in the report.

Keep an important gap at least about 6 pixels wide at the minimum 320-pixel raster presentation. This is a practical optical target for future commissions, not an assertion that all internal gaps have already been programmatically measured.

Prefer uneven weight and a purposeful interval over a perfectly centered badge. Do not fill unused space with dots, orbit rings, tiny mechanisms or corner marks. Those details compete with reading and can imply unsupported structure.

## Material and line

Use two compatible grammars: carved or folded solid forms, described with engraved pen contours. Ivory is the physical surface of the object; the surrounding page remains genuinely transparent. The drawing should suggest an object that can be turned in the mind, while remaining clearly illustrative.

Let the contour do most of the work. Secondary edges describe changes of plane. Hatching describes a turned surface or a cut face; it should not be an all-over noise field. Keep at least one large quiet surface in each composition.

For editable 1200-pixel vector masters, a useful initial hierarchy is an outer contour around 4-5 units, internal structural lines around 2-3 units, and sparse detail around 1-1.5 units. Inspect the rendered result rather than treating these ratios as guarantees. At 320 pixels, essential lines should survive and secondary texture may disappear.

Vary hatch direction with the plane being described. Concentrate detail at the focal event and let it fall away elsewhere. Avoid random roughness, decorative scratches, fuzzy pencil clouds and photoreal surface grain. Controlled variation should reveal material or depth.

A hole is real transparency, not a patch of the expected page colour. A visible rear wall is an actual surface, so it may be ivory. This distinction matters especially in cutaways: not every pale interior region is a failed transparent hole.

Do not put text into the artwork. Keep titles, equations, numbers, scale information, legends and explanatory labels selectable in the eventual host. The marks-and-ornaments owner retains annotation gestures, arrows and handwritten devices.

## Palette and theme

| Role | Light-context vector reference | Dark-context vector reference | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main contour | Graphite `#28343b` | Ivory `#f4f1e8` | Structure and silhouette |
| Physical plane | Warm ivory `#f4f1e8` | Resolved dark plane | Solid material, not page background |
| Focal accent | Vermilion `#b32624` | Pale red `#ffaaa5` | One named feature or change |
| Raster review matte | Paper `#f4f1e8` | Charcoal `#181c20` | Inspection surface only |

These are material and inspection references, not replacement application tokens. The current host keeps light/dark `--bg` at `#f4f1e9` / `#111312`, `--fg` at `#202c34` / `#ecebe6`, and the existing handwriting accent at `#b32624` / `#ffaaa5`. The marks and applications owners retain those host roles. Raster generation approximates palette colours and introduces tonal variation; do not claim exact spot colours from the prompt. The vector manifests record their actual resolved palette.

The separate vector audit boards use dark matte `#11171b`; this is an inspection surface recorded in their source, not a third application theme. Neither set of matte composites is a browser screenshot.

Give the red accent a physical location: a shim, an edge or a short section of the form. Never use red as a stand-in for “error,” “danger” or a measured intensity unless the source content establishes that meaning. The accent must also be identifiable by shape and position.

Preserve the raster originals across themes. Their ivory planes remain readable on both tested mattes. Do not automatically invert, recolour or apply blend modes to them. A future deliberate dark raster edition would be a new recorded revision, not a CSS effect applied to the master.

Vector light/dark/mono versions must preserve geometry, layer order and semantic relationships. Recolouring must not quietly change an opening or move an object. The mono version checks whether form still carries the idea when the red cue is removed.

## Scale

| Presentation width | Intended use | Required reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 640-960 px | Primary article illustration | Silhouette, focal relationship and some material detail |
| 320 px | Smallest intended raster illustration | Whole relationship remains intelligible; fine hatching is optional |
| 160 px | Contextual thumbnail with adjacent title | Subject and broad gesture only; no explanatory claim from detail |
| 64 px | Diagnostic export only for vectors | Not an approved category icon or essential explanatory figure |

Use contain sizing with the full canvas. Do not crop to cover, stretch one axis or automatically trim transparency. Large margins are part of the composition. Placement, responsive wrappers and captions belong to the application owner, so the packet supplies constraints without editing the host layout.

A tiny rendered image should become simpler in meaning, not denser in texture. If a future illustration relies on a thin arrow, a tiny red dot or several close parallel edges at 320 pixels, commission an optical revision rather than sharpening the same pixels.

## Narrative figure language

Choose the figure's claim before choosing its visual treatment.

| Narrative operation | Drawing rule | Caption obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare two states | Hold viewpoint, object identity and scale stable; make one intended difference visible | Name the changed property; avoid “better” or numeric implications without evidence |
| Reveal a structure | Keep cut boundaries, thickness and displaced pieces coherent | State whether the object is invented, schematic or a sourced section |
| Follow a transformation | Preserve continuity or clearly label discrete stages; show reading order without decorative arrows | State what changes and whether time, causality or physical feasibility is represented |
| Relate two systems | Draw the actual declared relation, with a selective connector when useful | Name the relation; proximity is not evidence of connection |
| Show a measured result | Use exact plotted data, labels, units, domains and a separate reproducible figure workflow | Cite the source, aggregation and uncertainty; do not use a generated illustration as the chart |

The three current raster studies exercise reveal and continuous-transformation grammar. EL01 is an exploded relationship, not a controlled before/after comparison. The comparison rule is reusable guidance; no additional comparison specimen is claimed.

These artworks are all illustrative. The vector counterparts are deterministic authored drawings with exact file geometry; that does not make their depicted objects physically exact. A mathematical diagram must separately state what is exact, sampled or approximate. A simulation requires its actual model and parameters. A data-derived figure needs its actual data. Do not promote a drawing from one class to another by giving it a technical title.

If a scientific analogy becomes misleading, change the analogy or its caption. Do not borrow the credibility of a laboratory plate through invented scale bars, equations, specimen numbers, topologies or vendor logos.

## Captions and access

Write alt text for what is visible. Put the interpretation and fidelity limit in a nearby caption. A useful pair for EL01 is:

- Alt: “Two pale stepped pieces sit apart, exposing a small red shim on the left piece.”
- Caption: “An invented assembly makes the interface visible. It illustrates a relationship between parts; it is not a dimensioned or functional joint.”

If an image only repeats adjacent prose and is truly decorative in its final context, the application owner may choose empty alt text. That decision depends on the actual page, not on the file extension.

Do not rely on red alone to explain the focal event. Retain the caption in monochrome print and accessible reading modes. Test any host labels separately for contrast, font size and focus; these specimens do not validate a browser implementation.

## Why this family belongs

The existing eight editorial PNGs provide a coherent material baseline: ivory surfaces, graphite contours, limited hatching and one red physical detail. The new work changes the relationship and canvas, rather than replacing that family or renaming existing objects.

The beneb direction contributes mass, purposeful clearance and strong negative space. Its dark futuristic logo studies remain their own collection; a historical logo selection is not approval of these illustrations. The blog's later folio guidance controls the warm reading context.

EL01 is the clearest opener because the eye finds the interval immediately. EL02 offers a richer section study, with the risk that readers could mistake an invented shell for anatomy. EL03 creates a reading gesture and is useful where the prose discusses progressive construction; it must not be called a folding proof.

Retain all three as a proposed grammar set. Refine only a concrete defect or a user-selected direction. Do not expand into more subjects until the relationship, scale and fidelity contract for the next article are known.
