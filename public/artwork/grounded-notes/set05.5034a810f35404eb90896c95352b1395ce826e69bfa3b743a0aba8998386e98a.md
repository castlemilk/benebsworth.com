# Grounded forms · Set 05 · Plant exchange

Source and review date: 2026-11-14 UTC. These are original illustrative biological studies, not traced diagrams, species portraits, micrographs, measured models or scientific simulations. University and textbook references supply facts only. No source image or figure geometry is copied. Clear areas within tissue are an engraving/cutaway convention; they do not claim that a cell is biologically empty.

## 01 · Stomatal gate
Two guard cells flank a leaf stoma and regulate its opening. The open space between them is the focal biological relationship. Leaf epidermal boundaries provide context. Ten enlarged organelle outlines are illustrative chloroplasts; their count and arrangement are not biological measurements. Guard-cell curvature, pore width, wall thickness and epidermal shapes are invented for the engraving. No turgor calculation or species diagnosis is implied.

Reference: [OpenStax Biology 2e, 30.2 Stems](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-2-stems), leaf epidermis and stomatal guard-cell discussion. Chloroplast detail: [University of Auckland, The Leaf](https://www.nzplants.auckland.ac.nz/en/about/resources/animations/the-leaf.html). Chloroplast-bearing guard cells are a common relationship, not a claim that every species has them.

## 02 · Root hair zone
Root hairs are extensions of epidermal cells and occur in the maturation region behind the growing root tip. They increase the surface available for water and mineral uptake. Here sixteen unbranched hairs attach above an unhairy distal region and protective root cap. Hair width and spacing are enlarged, internal cell files are schematic, and the root is not a measured anatomical section. No transport chemistry or taxonomic claim is made.

Reference: [OpenStax Biology 2e, 30.3 Roots](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-3-roots), root anatomy and maturation zone.

## 03 · Xylem vessel
Vessel elements join end to end, with end-wall perforations allowing longitudinal water movement. The drawing opens the front wall of two aligned elements to reveal their continuous lumen and a simple perforation. Lignified wall mass is emphasized. Small side-wall marks stand for pits with thin-wall/membrane regions; they are not drilled holes through the wall. The spacing and exaggerated wall thickness are illustrative. No hydraulic performance or universal xylem pit pattern is claimed.

Reference: [OpenStax Biology 2e, 30.2 Stems](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/30-2-stems), xylem tissue, vessel elements and perforation plates. Pit-membrane detail: [University of Vigo, Atlas of Plant and Animal Histology: Cell wall](https://mmegias.webs.uvigo.es/02-english/5-celulas/ampliaciones/2-pared-celular.php). The pit membrane is primary-wall material, not a living lipid membrane.

## 04 · Pollen tube
A pollen tube grows from a germinating pollen grain and conveys sperm cells. In flowering plants two sperm are delivered toward the ovule. This isolated study shows two schematic sperm cells and a separate tube nucleus within a continuous tube. Stigma, style and ovule are omitted. Exine sculpture, nuclear positions and relative sizes are invented for visibility. This does not illustrate a complete fertilization event or a species-specific developmental time point.

References: [OpenStax Biology 2e, 26.3 Angiosperms](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/26-3-angiosperms); [Georgia Tech Organismal Biology, Plant Reproduction](https://organismalbio.biosci.gatech.edu/growth-and-reproduction/plant-reproduction/); [Gilbert, Developmental Biology, Gamete Production in Angiosperms](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10129/), tube nucleus and sperm formation.

## 05 · Fern sorus
A sorus is a group of sporangia on a fern frond. Many leptosporangiate sporangia have an annulus of specialized thickened cells involved in opening and spore dispersal. Five enlarged stalked sporangia are shown here on a leaf-underside fragment, one open. The five capsules, fifteen annulus segments per capsule, and small visible spore count are illustrative choices, not universal biological counts. An indusium is omitted. Capsule spacing is expanded so the attachments can be seen. No dispersal dynamics are simulated.

References: [University of British Columbia, Fern Sporangia](https://blogs.ubc.ca/biology210/lab/lab-7-ferns/fern-sporophyte/fern-sporangia/); [Chinese University of Hong Kong, Virtual Lab of Ferns](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/lifesciences/vl/fern/fern_structure_vlabel_4.html).

## 10 Cnidocyte discharge

# Grounded Forms 05 · Light and waves

Checked 2026-11-14 UTC. These are original-authored vector studies, not copied textbook figures, measured datasets, calibrated instruments, or engineering drawings. Primary textbook sources supplied factual principles only. All source figures and layouts were excluded. Coordinates are illustration units, serialized to four decimal places. Hyperbola and sine contours are sampled polylines; the parabola, circles, and ellipses use native analytic SVG primitives. Display captions must preserve the limits below.

## 11 · Parabolic reflector

**Basis:** Parallel incident rays reflect toward one focus on a parabolic cross-section. [OpenStax, University Physics III, 2.2 Spherical Mirrors](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-2-spherical-mirrors).

**Our construction:** The exact section is x = 235 + h²/(4f), y = 430 + h, with f = 155. Its focus is (390, 430). One quadratic Bézier represents the parabola exactly. Eight incoming directions are (-1, 0). The reflection calculation uses r = d − 2(d·n)n, with n proportional to (1, −h/(2f)). Each reflected direction is tested against the direction to the focus.

**Limit:** Geometric optics, on-axis parallel illumination, ideal specular surface. The backing, pedestal, arrows, focal marker, and dimension bracket are illustrative. Neither diffraction nor a complete three-dimensional dish is claimed.

## 12 · Double-slit interference

**Basis:** Two monochromatic coherent narrow apertures can interfere constructively when their path difference is an integer wavelength. [OpenStax, University Physics III, 3.1 Young’s Double-Slit Interference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/3-1-youngs-double-slit-interference).

**Our construction:** Apertures are at (235, 392) and (235, 508); separation d = 116, wavelength λ = 18, receiver distance L = 465. For δ = mλ, a = |δ|/2 and b² = (d/2)² − a² give the exact locus y = sign(δ)a√(1+x²/b²), relative to the aperture midpoint. Solid curves show m = −3…3; dashed curves show intervening half-orders. Each hyperbola has 121 sampled vertices. Receiver bar lengths sample I/Imax = cos²(πΔr/λ), using exact path distances; values at or below 0.008 are omitted.

**Limit:** Curves are equal-path-difference loci, **not light rays or trajectories**. This ideal equal-amplitude scalar model omits spreading losses, finite-slit diffraction envelopes, and polarization. The receiver bars are calculated relative intensity, not experimental data.

## 13 · Linear polarization

**Basis:** A plane electromagnetic wave is transverse, with polarization conventionally specified by its electric-field direction. [OpenStax, University Physics III, 1.7 Polarization](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-7-polarization).

**Our construction:** In real model coordinates, k is along x and E(x) = (0, 0, A sin(2πx/λ)), with A = 96, λ = 210, and length 630. The projection sends propagation positions to (140+x, 610−3x/7) and electric-field vectors vertically on the page. The same field function passes continuously through the aligned ideal analyzer. Fine arrows are sampled electric-field vectors; the 181-vertex curve samples the continuous tip envelope. The outside double arrow identifies the analyzer’s transmission axis.

**Limit:** This is a projected field diagram, **not photon or particle motion**. Apparent screen angles are not the real-space orthogonality test. The magnetic field is omitted. The frame is illustrative and is not a wire-grid polarizer or specified material.

## 14 · Doppler wavefronts

**Basis:** A source moving through a stationary medium compresses forward wavefront spacing and increases rear spacing. [OpenStax, University Physics I, 17.7 The Doppler Effect](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-7-the-doppler-effect).

**Our construction:** The present point source is (590, 430), with Mach ratio M = vs/c = 0.55 and cT = 45. Six fronts have radii rn = 45n and centers (590 − 0.55rn, 430). The entire section is rotated −16 degrees. Thus forward spacing is 20.25 and rear spacing is 69.75; the on-axis bracket lengths encode those values. Small outlined dots identify earlier emission positions. Copper arcs are highlighted parts of the same circles, not additional fronts.

**Limit:** A two-dimensional section of spherical acoustic phase fronts in a stationary homogeneous medium. The source symbol and velocity arrow are illustrative. No relativistic optical Doppler model, wake, shock, or intensity decay is claimed.

## 15 · Pipe resonance

**Basis:** Ideal open ends are displacement antinodes; a rigid closed end is a displacement node. Open/open and open/closed fundamentals fit a half and quarter wavelength respectively. [OpenStax, University Physics I, 17.5 Sources of Musical Sound](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-5-sources-of-musical-sound).

**Our construction:** The left pipe has open/open length Lo = 520; the right has closed/open length Lc = 260. They share λ = 1040 and f = c/1040. Left displacement shape is u(x) = A cos(πx/Lo), measured downward from its top; right shape is u(x) = A sin(πx/(2Lc)), measured upward from its closed bottom. A = 25. Double-headed **axial** arrows show ±|u| at fixed stations, and small rings identify zero displacement. They show amplitude envelopes, not one simultaneous signed snapshot.

**Limit:** One-dimensional, lossless, ideal pressure-release open ends and a rigid closed end. End correction, radiation losses, bore-dependent behavior, and drive mechanism are omitted. The finite drawn bore width is illustrative. These are longitudinal modes, not transverse membrane modes.


# Mechanics in balance

These five studies are original illustrations, not manufacturing drawings or experimental results. Equations govern the stated relationships; vessels, supports, thicknesses and engraved surfaces are illustrative. Engraving gaps are real transparent areas in the artwork, not necessarily physical holes in the represented object.

## 16 · Primed siphon

A constant-bore tube is filled with liquid. Its inlet is below the upstream free surface; the open outlet is lower than that free surface and above the receiver's liquid. Both exposed surfaces are at the same atmospheric pressure. For steady incompressible, inviscid flow with negligible source-reservoir velocity, the ideal outlet speed is v = √(2gΔh). The drawn height difference is 220 units, representing 0.88 m at 0.004 m/unit. With g = 9.81 m/s² this gives approximately 4.16 m/s. The apex pressure is calculated from the same Bernoulli model; it is not a displayed pressure measurement.

This is a primed-flow snapshot. Startup, losses, air ingress, cavitation and changing reservoir levels are not modeled. The free-jet contours are illustrative, not a solved trajectory.

Source: [Duke Physics, simple siphon problem](https://webhome.phy.duke.edu/~rgb/siphon). Principles and equations only; no figure copied.

## 17 · Pressure head

A gas reservoir communicates with the left side of a U-tube; the right side is open to atmosphere. A single liquid at rest supports positive left-side gauge pressure. The interface centers differ by 170 SVG units, representing 0.17 m. For liquid density 1000 kg/m³ and uniform g = 9.81 m/s², p_left − p_right = ρgΔh = 1667.7 Pa.

Constant density, hydrostatic equilibrium and negligible gas density are assumed. Surface tension and capillary corrections are omitted. The curved menisci and wall thickness are schematic. The arrow in the gas limb indicates applied pressure, not measured fluid velocity.

Source: [OpenStax, Measuring Pressure](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-2-measuring-pressure). Facts only; no source drawing reused.

## 18 · Floating prism

This is an orthographic explanatory section of a uniform rectangular prism with constant depth out of the page. Half its height, and therefore half its volume, lies below the free surface. A solid-to-liquid density ratio of 0.5 satisfies static Archimedes balance: the weight equals the weight of displaced liquid. The two equally long arrows compare net buoyancy and weight magnitudes; their separated locations do not assert the true points of application.

Tilt, stability, waves, air buoyancy, capillarity and porosity are omitted. The wood-like engraving is invented, not measured material grain. Blank areas inside the block represent engraving, not a hollow block.

Source: [OpenStax, Archimedes’ Principle and Buoyancy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy).

## 19 · Slider-crank

An in-line slider is connected to a crank through a rigid rod. Relative to the fixed crank pivot, the selected open-branch slider coordinate is x = r cos θ + √(L² − r² sin² θ), y = 0. The illustration uses r = 128 SVG units, L = 350 and θ = 54°. The crank pin, connecting rod and slider pin satisfy exact center-to-center closure before coordinates are rounded for SVG. Full rotation gives stroke 2r = 256 units; this is not a pure sinusoidal slider trajectory.

The wheel, stand, guides and fasteners are original illustrative hardware. Only position closure is validated, not force, torque, collision clearance, material strength or manufacture.

Source: [Aykut C. Satici, Position-Level Analysis](https://symplectomorphism.github.io/teaching/ug_courses/kinematics/chapter06.html).

## 20 · Centrifugal governor

A symmetric Watt-type governor uses equal upper and lower arms, with a sliding sleeve on the central shaft. The two ball centers are a side projection of one diametric pair in steady rotation. With arm length L and angle θ from vertical, radius is L sin θ, governor height is L cos θ, and sleeve distance below the upper pivot is 2L cos θ. For negligible sleeve/link mass and point-mass flyballs, conical-pendulum balance gives ω² = g/(L cos θ). The stated illustrative values are L = 0.4 m and θ = 35°. The elliptical arc indicates rotation; it is not another linkage.

No valve, transient response, control stability, safe operating speed or historical-machine reconstruction is claimed. Friction, link weight and sleeve load are omitted.

Sources: [Richard Fitzpatrick, UT Austin, Spherical Pendulum](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/336k/Newton/node82.html) for equilibrium; [Science Museum Group, Boulton and Watt rotative engine](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co50948/rotative-steam-engine-by-boulton-and-watt-1788) for historical context only. No collection imagery or machine design was copied.

## 21 · Cycloid trace

A circle of radius r rolling without slipping along a straight line produces the rim-point locus x=r(t−sin t), y=r(1−cos t). One arch is shown for 0≤t≤2π, r=100, with a circle snapshot at t=4.45. The screen rotates this complete geometry by −14°; the rail is the straight rolling line, not a claim about gravitational dynamics. The curve is a 320-segment approximation, while its circle is a native SVG circle. The spokes, rail engraving and accent sector are illustrative. Maximum measured midpoint chord error is below 0.005 viewBox units.

Source: [OpenStax, Calculus Volume 2, §7.1](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/7-1-parametric-equations).

## 22 · Hilbert channel

The delivered finite order-three traversal visits every vertex of an 8×8 integer grid exactly once, using 63 unit-length adjacent steps. An affine projection establishes the oblique viewpoint. Two mitered walls, a shallow side bevel and end cuffs give the traversal an illustrative channel construction. These added widths are not part of the mathematical curve. This finite polygonal path is not itself space-filling; that property belongs to the limiting construction.

Source: [J. M. Spivey, University of Oxford, GeomLab worksheet 7](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/geomlab/sheet7.html).

## 23 · Ruled saddle

The model is z=0.7xy, restricted to the annulus 0.36²≤x²+y²≤1.23². Holding either x or y constant gives a straight generator, so the two visible families are truly straight in three dimensions and remain straight under the stated linear projection. The outer boundary and the inner editing aperture are sampled at 301 positions. The hole is a selected domain cut, not a hole inherent to a complete hyperbolic paraboloid. Projection remains nonfolding throughout the delivered domain. This is a mathematical cut-surface, not a tested tensile structure or building proposal.

Source: [Joel Roberts, University of Minnesota, A hyperbolic paraboloid](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~rober005/java.dir/JGV/paraboloid1.html).

## 24 · Constant-width gauge

Three equal-radius circular arcs, centered on the vertices of an equilateral triangle, form the exterior Reuleaux boundary. Its width is the side length of that triangle. Horizontal supporting lines touch the outer profile at a separation of 385 units in the main study and 120 units in each of the two smaller studies. The small studies have equal scale to one another, different orientations and independently calculated vertical translations. They are static examples, not frames of a fixed-axis rolling wheel. No axle, circular rotation center or constant axle height is implied. The central opening is an ink/aperture treatment of the profile; the geometric constant-width claim concerns its convex exterior, not the perforated ink region. Numerical support checks use exact endpoint/stationary-point candidates on the circular arcs, not sampled extrema.

Source: [Stefan Sechelmann, TU Berlin Polyhedral Surfaces, The Reuleaux Triangle Tetrahedron](https://sechel.de/reuleauxApplet.html).

## 25 · Polyhedral dual

The cube has vertices (±1,±1,±1). Its face centers are (±1,0,0), (0,±1,0), (0,0,±1); joining centers of adjacent cube faces constructs a regular octahedron with twelve edges of length √2. One orthographic camera looking from direction (4,5,3) controls both solids. Cube rear edges are dashed, front edges are solid and can cross in front of the octahedron. Hidden octahedral edges are omitted. Four visible facets are inset slightly as ink panels, leaving true transparent seams; exact boundary edges preserve the projected model. Two diagonals of the cube's upper face meet at the dual's upper vertex. This is a projected geometric relationship, not an arbitrary ball-and-stick network.

Source: [Thomas Banchoff, Brown University, Coordinates for Regular Polyhedra](https://www.math.brown.edu/tbanchof/Beyond3d/chapter8/section05.html).
