## 17 · Brewster interface

**Basis.** For hypothetical refractive indices n1=1 and n2=1.5, Brewster incidence is atan(1.5), about 56.31 degrees from the normal. Snell refraction is about 33.69 degrees, making the reflected and transmitted directions perpendicular. The ideal p-polarized reflection amplitude is zero; unpolarized incident light therefore yields an s-polarized reflected component.

**Limits.** Planar geometric-optics interface between ideal lossless, nonmagnetic isotropic media with hypothetical indices 1 and 1.5. For unpolarized incident light, the reflected component is s-polarized; circle-dot glyphs beside the reflected ray denote electric-field direction perpendicular to the drawing plane, not discrete particles or measured amplitude. Transmitted light is not claimed to be purely p-polarized. Line weights do not encode power; absorption, dispersion and finite interfaces are omitted.

**Provenance.** Original authored editable vector geometry; ideal mathematical optical relations with explicitly illustrative apparatus. No copied, traced or embedded reference imagery.

## 20 · Corner retroreflector

**Basis.** A planar ray first reflects from x=330 and then from y=650. Its normalized direction changes from (-0.8,0.6) to (0.8,0.6) and finally to (0.8,-0.6). The two perpendicular reflections reverse the input direction exactly while offsetting the output line. This is a two-mirror cross-section, not a three-dimensional cube corner.

**Limits.** Ideal two-dimensional specular reflection. Rays strike each mirror once and emerge antiparallel, with a lateral offset. Two perpendicular mirrors do not reverse arbitrary three-dimensional ray directions; a trihedral cube corner requires a third mirror. Finite coating, diffraction, losses, alignment tolerance and mount design are not modeled. Line weight does not encode intensity.
