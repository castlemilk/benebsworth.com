## 11 · Poiseuille profile

A broad pair of engraved pipe walls frames a bow-shaped speed envelope. Nine arrows grow toward the axis; a detached circular section establishes the tube rather than a flat channel.

**Basis.** For steady, fully developed laminar flow of an incompressible Newtonian fluid in a straight circular tube, u(r)=G(R²-r²)/(4mu), where G=-dp/dx. The drawn profile and arrow lengths follow this parabola. A hypothetical R=0.005 m, mu=0.001 Pa s, density 1000 kg/m³ and G=32 Pa/m give maximum speed 0.2 m/s, mean speed 0.1 m/s and diameter-based Reynolds number 1000.

**Limits.** A meridional section and separate circular-section key, with editorial wall thickness and independent display scales. Assumes no slip, a uniform circular tube and fully developed laminar flow. Entrance effects, turbulence, non-Newtonian behavior, compressibility and wall deformation are omitted. This is not a physiological or pipe-design prediction.

**Provenance.** Original authored vector geometry. Factual source principles inform the limited model; no source figure or photograph is traced or reproduced.

**Minimum display width:** 320 px.

- [UC Berkeley: Flow Rate, Poiseuille’s Law](https://ucb-bioe.github.io/textbook/chapters/05_diagnostics/07_poiseuille.html). Factual principles only. No source image, figure or extended text reproduced; no source-image licence claimed.

## 15 · Rankine vortex

Broken circular streamlines wrap an off-center core, leaving a clean lower-right sector. Five tangential arrows rise and then shorten; a detached kinked profile contrasts solid-body rotation with inverse-radius decay.

**Basis.** The ideal Rankine vortex has tangential speed v=Omega r within core radius a and v=Omega a²/r outside it. **Limits.** An ideal inviscid two-dimensional vortex, not a viscously evolving flow, tornado forecast or draining bathtub. Free-surface depression, axial flow, external strain and viscous diffusion are not modeled.
