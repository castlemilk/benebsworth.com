  <h1>System: x&sup2; + y&sup2; = 25 &nbsp;and&nbsp; x&middot;y = 12</h1>
  <p class="sub">All real solution pairs, with full algebraic justification.</p>

  <h2>Setup</h2>
  <div class="card">
    <div class="eq">(1)&nbsp;&nbsp;x&sup2; + y&sup2; = 25</div>
    <div class="eq">(2)&nbsp;&nbsp;x&middot;y = 12</div>
    <p>Since <span class="m">xy = 12 &ne; 0</span>, neither variable is zero, and
    <span class="m">x</span> and <span class="m">y</span> must have the <em>same sign</em>
    (their product is positive). Geometrically: equation (1) is the circle of radius 5 centered
    at the origin; equation (2) is a rectangular hyperbola in quadrants I and III. Intersections
    can only occur in those two quadrants.</p>
  </div>

  <h2>Step 1 &mdash; Recover the sum x + y</h2>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="step-label">Identity</span>
    <p>Use the square-of-a-sum identity and substitute (1) and (2):</p>
    <div class="eq">(x + y)&sup2; = x&sup2; + 2xy + y&sup2; = (x&sup2; + y&sup2;) + 2(xy) = <span class="hl">25 + 2&middot;12 = 49</span></div>
    <p>Taking square roots over the reals:</p>
    <div class="eq res">x + y = &plusmn;7</div>
    <p class="note">The sign alternatives are consistent with Step 0: the product is positive,
    so x and y share a sign &mdash; both positive (sum +7) or both negative (sum &minus;7).</p>
  </div>

  <h2>Step 2 &mdash; Reduce to a quadratic via Vieta&rsquo;s formulas</h2>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="step-label">Vieta</span>
    <p>If two numbers have sum <span class="m">s</span> and product <span class="m">p</span>, they are
    exactly the roots of <span class="m">t&sup2; &minus; s&middot;t + p = 0</span>. Here
    <span class="m">p = xy = 12</span> and <span class="m">s = x + y = &plusmn;7</span>.</p>

    <p><strong>Case A: s = +7</strong></p>
    <div class="eq">t&sup2; &minus; 7t + 12 = 0 &nbsp;&rArr;&nbsp; (t &minus; 3)(t &minus; 4) = 0 &nbsp;&rArr;&nbsp; t &isin; {3, 4}</div>
    <p>So <span class="m">{x, y} = {3, 4}</span>, giving the ordered pairs
    <span class="res">(3, 4)</span> and <span class="res">(4, 3)</span>.</p>

    <p><strong>Case B: s = &minus;7</strong></p>
    <div class="eq">t&sup2; + 7t + 12 = 0 &nbsp;&rArr;&nbsp; (t + 3)(t + 4) = 0 &nbsp;&rArr;&nbsp; t &isin; {&minus;3, &minus;4}</div>
    <p>So <span class="m">{x, y} = {&minus;3, &minus;4}</span>, giving the ordered pairs
    <span class="res">(&minus;3, &minus;4)</span> and <span class="res">(&minus;4, &minus;3)</span>.</p>
  </div>

  <h2>Step 2&prime; &mdash; Cross-check via the difference</h2>
  <div class="card">
    <span class="step-label">Check</span>
    <p>The companion identity gives an independent confirmation:</p>
    <div class="eq">(x &minus; y)&sup2; = x&sup2; &minus; 2xy + y&sup2; = 25 &minus; 24 = 1 &nbsp;&rArr;&nbsp; x &minus; y = &plusmn;1</div>
    <p>Solving the linear system <span class="m">x + y = 7, x &minus; y = &plusmn;1</span> yields
    <span class="m">(x, y) = (4, 3)</span> or <span class="m">(3, 4)</span>; the sum &minus;7
    yields <span class="m">(&minus;4, &minus;3)</span> or <span class="m">(&minus;3, &minus;4)</span> —
    the same four pairs.</p>
  </div>

  <h2>Step 3 &mdash; Verify every candidate in the original system</h2>
  <div class="card">
    <table>
      <tr><th>(x, y)</th><th>x&sup2; + y&sup2;</th><th>x&middot;y</th><th>Valid?</th></tr>
      <tr><td>(3, 4)</td><td>9 + 16 = 25</td><td>12</td><td style="color:var(--ok)">&#10003;</td></tr>
      <tr><td>(4, 3)</td><td>16 + 9 = 25</td><td>12</td><td style="color:var(--ok)">&#10003;</td></tr>
      <tr><td>(&minus;3, &minus;4)</td><td>9 + 16 = 25</td><td>12</td><td style="color:var(--ok)">&#10003;</td></tr>
      <tr><td>(&minus;4, &minus;3)</td><td>16 + 9 = 25</td><td>12</td><td style="color:var(--ok)">&#10003;</td></tr>
    </table>
    <p class="note"><strong>Completeness:</strong> every step was reversible (identities, square
    root over &#8477;, Vieta), so no solutions were lost or introduced: the derivation shows any
    solution must have <span class="m">x + y = &plusmn;7</span>, and each case produces exactly
    the pairs listed. Hence this is the complete solution set — consistent with a circle and a
    hyperbola meeting in at most 4 points.</p>
  </div>

  <h2>Answer</h2>
  <div class="card final">
    <div class="eq">(x, y) &isin; { (3, 4),&nbsp; (4, 3),&nbsp; (&minus;3, &minus;4),&nbsp; (&minus;4, &minus;3) }</div>
    <p style="text-align:center" class="note">Exactly four real solutions — the symmetric
    pairs where |x| and |y| are 3 and 4 with matching signs.</p>
  </div>
