  <h1>Solve the system over the reals</h1>
  <p class="equation">x² + y² = 25, &nbsp; xy = 12.</p>
  <div class="answer">
    <p><strong>Every real solution pair (x, y):</strong></p>
    <p class="equation">{(4, 3), (3, 4), (−3, −4), (−4, −3)}.</p>
  </div>
  <h2>1. Find the sum and the difference</h2>
  <p>Expand the squares and substitute the two given equations:</p>
  <p class="equation">(x + y)² = x² + y² + 2xy = 25 + 24 = 49,</p>
  <p class="equation">(x − y)² = x² + y² − 2xy = 25 − 24 = 1.</p>
  <p>For a real number, a square equal to 49 has exactly the roots 7 and −7, and a square equal to 1 has exactly the roots 1 and −1. Thus</p>
  <p class="equation">x + y ∈ {7, −7}, &nbsp; x − y ∈ {1, −1}.</p>
  <h2>2. Recover x and y for each sign choice</h2>
  <p>Let s = x + y and d = x − y. Adding these equations gives 2x = s + d; subtracting the second from the first gives 2y = s − d. Dividing by the nonzero constant 2 yields</p>
  <p class="equation">x = (s + d)/2, &nbsp; y = (s − d)/2.</p>
  <table>
    <caption>All four choices for (s, d)</caption>
    <thead><tr><th scope="col">s</th><th scope="col">d</th><th scope="col">x</th><th scope="col">y</th><th scope="col">(x, y)</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>7</td><td>1</td><td>4</td><td>3</td><td>(4, 3)</td></tr>
      <tr><td>7</td><td>−1</td><td>3</td><td>4</td><td>(3, 4)</td></tr>
      <tr><td>−7</td><td>1</td><td>−3</td><td>−4</td><td>(−3, −4)</td></tr>
      <tr><td>−7</td><td>−1</td><td>−4</td><td>−3</td><td>(−4, −3)</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
  <h2>3. Verify the solutions and completeness</h2>
  <p>For every sign choice above, s² = 49 and d² = 1. Substituting the formulas for x and y back into the original expressions gives</p>
  <p class="equation">x² + y² = [(s + d)² + (s − d)²]/4<br>= (s² + d²)/2 = (49 + 1)/2 = 25,</p>
  <p class="equation">xy = (s + d)(s − d)/4<br>= (s² − d²)/4 = (49 − 1)/4 = 12.</p>
  <p>Hence all four listed pairs satisfy both equations. Every real solution must produce one of the four listed combinations of s and d, and each combination determines exactly one pair (x, y). Therefore there are no other real solutions.</p>
